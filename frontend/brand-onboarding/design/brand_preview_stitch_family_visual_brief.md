# Brand Preview — Stitch Screen-Family Visual Brief

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** PROPOSED VISUAL DIRECTION — READY FOR REVIEW  
**Owner:** UI/UX Design AI Worker  
**Scope:** Remaining Brand Preview Stitch families only  
**Applies to:** `BP-ST-M03`, `BP-ST-D01`, `BP-ST-M01`, `BP-ST-D02`, `BP-ST-M02`

## 1. Purpose

This brief translates the frozen Brand Preview visual direction into concrete screen-family guidance for the remaining Stitch references.

It does **not** reopen or modify:

- Product semantics;
- frozen static copy;
- dynamic-copy constraints;
- frontend state meaning;
- screen order;
- readiness rules;
- interaction permissions;
- retry/verification authority;
- Aurora design-system authority.

The accepted Brand Preview Desktop lineage is useful evidence of direction, but its individual visual choices are not all permanent authority. The remaining families should inherit its strongest qualities — calm hierarchy, useful editorial emphasis, restrained surfaces — without mechanically copying every card, spacing or ornament decision.

---

## 2. Shared visual principle

Across all remaining Brand Preview families, the visual world should feel:

```text
calm
intelligent
premium through restraint
human
trustworthy
specific to Creator Shop
continuous with onboarding
```

Avoid the visual grammar of:

```text
technical scanning
analytics dashboards
administrative review
status consoles
generic SaaS cards
generic loading/error pages
```

Aurora remains the primitive/component/token authority. This brief governs composition, hierarchy, rhythm, emphasis and visual character.

# 3. Family C — Brand Preview Mobile 390

## 3.1 Intended user feeling

The user should feel a sequence of increasingly valuable recognition:

```text
They found my brand
→ They understand it
→ They understand who matters
→ They can see where creators help
→ They already have a useful creator point of view
→ I want to continue
```

The mobile page should feel like a **guided reveal**, not a long report compressed into one column.

## 3.2 Dominant visual moment

The dominant moment is the early combination of:

1. compact Brand Preview reveal;
2. immediate brand recognition;
3. the `How we understand your brand` insight surface.

A meaningful portion of this value should be visible within the first mobile viewport or strongly signalled immediately below it. Do not spend the opening viewport on oversized title spacing, decorative art or metadata.

## 3.3 Hierarchy

Priority order:

1. Brand Preview reveal headline;
2. recognizable brand identity;
3. Brand-understanding narrative as the intellectual anchor;
4. creator opportunities as the strongest strategic/actionable section;
5. Audience cohorts;
6. creator archetypes;
7. verification continuation.

The canonical semantic order remains unchanged. Visual emphasis may differ inside that order.

## 3.4 Spatial rhythm

Use a deliberate cadence:

```text
compact reveal
→ recognition pause
→ strong insight surface
→ breathing space
→ strategic cohort sequence
→ stronger opportunity sequence
→ lighter human creator sequence
→ integrated verification ending
```

Use spacing changes and surface transitions to mark chapters. Do not rely on identical card spacing throughout the entire scroll.

## 3.5 Preferred composition patterns

- Keep the header compact enough to reveal meaningful content early.
- Treat brand recognition as a compact identity row/block rather than a large metadata card.
- Give the Brand-understanding narrative one unmistakable feature treatment.
- Audience items may use restrained cohort rows/cards with compact semantic markers.
- Opportunities should feel more directional than Audience: stronger title emphasis, clearer visual entry point, but no Campaign controls.
- Archetypes should feel lighter and more human than report cards; use label/rationale rhythm and restrained semantic iconography.
- Verification should feel like the concluding chapter of the same page, not a detached conversion banner.

## 3.6 Card / surface strategy

Do not simply stack Desktop cards at full width.

Prefer:

- one strong insight surface;
- lighter list/row treatments for some repeated items;
- section-level grouping through spacing and background/surface shifts;
- minimal nested borders;
- no empty reserved slots at reduced density.

A one-item Audience/Opportunity/Archetype section must look intentionally concise.

## 3.7 Typography emphasis

- Headline: strong but not oversized enough to consume the first viewport.
- Brand name: visually prominent inside recognition.
- Brand-understanding narrative: comfortable reading width, normal Aurora body size, generous line height.
- Opportunity titles: strongest repeated-item title treatment.
- Helper copy: visibly secondary; do not let it compete with dynamic content.
- Archetype labels: memorable, concise, slightly stronger than rationale text.

Never shrink body copy to reduce scroll length.

## 3.8 Icon / illustration role

Use restrained icons only when they improve semantic recognition between Audience, Opportunity and Archetype content.

Do not introduce:

- healthcare-themed illustration;
- persona portraits;
- decorative AI imagery;
- large hero illustration competing with the insight reveal.

## 3.9 Whitespace / density

Mobile should feel **lighter than Desktop**, not denser.

- Use compact spacing within repeated items.
- Use more generous separation between semantic chapters.
- Avoid giant dead zones.
- Allow long bounded copy to expand naturally.
- Avoid excessive borders/shadows that make a long page visually heavy.

## 3.10 Desktop → mobile transformation guidance

Preserve the Desktop visual language, not its literal geometry.

Transform by:

- compressing the reveal;
- simplifying recognition;
- retaining the insight anchor;
- converting repeated grids into deliberate vertical sequences;
- reducing card heaviness;
- increasing chapter-level rhythm;
- preserving the in-flow CTA ending.

Do not use horizontal carousels.

## 3.11 CTA prominence

`Verify & claim this brand` should become clearly dominant only at the natural end of the value story.

The canonical in-flow transition remains required. A sticky equivalent, if Stitch explores it under existing FE/Product permission, must stay visually quiet enough that it does not compete with the Preview while the user is still reading.

## 3.12 Must not resemble

- a vertically stacked desktop dashboard;
- a long document of identical cards;
- a marketing landing page with a persistent conversion bar;
- an onboarding wizard;
- a healthcare portal;
- a mobile report/PDF.

## 3.13 Visual acceptance checks

1. Within the opening viewport, the screen communicates both **personalized Brand Preview** and recognizable brand context without excessive title whitespace.
2. `How we understand your brand` is visually identifiable as the primary insight, not merely the second card in a sequence.
3. Audience, Opportunities and Archetypes do not all use the same repeated-card treatment.
4. The vertical rhythm clearly preserves `recognition → understanding → audience clarity → creator opportunity → archetypes → continuation` without truncating copy.
5. The verification ending feels integrated and decisive without turning the whole page into a conversion funnel.

# 4. Family A — Fast Brand Analysis Desktop

## 4.1 Intended user feeling

> **Creator Shop is forming an understanding of my brand.**

The user should feel calm anticipation and visible intellectual progress, not uncertainty about a technical process.

## 4.2 Dominant visual moment

A **single evolving understanding signature** should dominate the screen.

Recommended visual concept:

### The Understanding Thread

Represent the truthful semantic phases as one calm progression from brand recognition toward the eventual Brand Preview. This may take the form of a restrained vertical or horizontal sequence of semantic statements connected by a subtle visual thread, pulse, reveal, or Aurora accent transition.

The signature should communicate:

```text
brand
→ audience
→ creator opportunity
→ Preview
```

without implying numeric completion.

It should feel like ideas are becoming clearer, not like files/pages are being crawled.

## 4.3 Hierarchy

1. analysis headline;
2. concise support copy;
3. Understanding Thread / current truthful phase;
4. compact brand/domain recognition cue;
5. slow-state reassurance only when applicable.

Do not make four phases four equal dashboard cards.

## 4.4 Spatial rhythm

Desktop should have calm focus rather than lots of content.

Use a concentrated central or slightly offset composition with purposeful negative space around the analysis signature. The screen can breathe, but it should not feel empty or like a generic waiting room.

## 4.5 Preferred composition patterns

Preferred options include:

- an editorial text-led analysis block paired with the Understanding Thread;
- a quiet brand-recognition anchor followed by progressive semantic statements;
- a single contained analysis surface with internal hierarchy rather than multiple status tiles.

The current truthful phase should be immediately legible. Completed phases may recede rather than displaying celebratory success states.

## 4.6 Card / surface strategy

Use at most one principal analysis surface if a surface is needed.

Avoid:

- one card per phase;
- metric tiles;
- nested status cards;
- terminal-style panels;
- large progress bars.

## 4.7 Typography emphasis

Typography should carry the sense of intelligence forming:

- headline confident and calm;
- current phase noticeably stronger;
- completed phases quieter but still readable;
- future phases visually present only if the chosen truthful-state treatment permits them without implying false progress;
- support copy secondary.

## 4.8 Icon / illustration role

Icons are optional and should be semantic rather than technical.

Acceptable roles:

- subtle brand recognition marker;
- restrained semantic markers for brand / audience / creator opportunity / Preview.

Avoid:

- magnifying glass scan animations;
- crawling/web/network diagrams;
- CPU/AI brain motifs;
- radar;
- rotating loader as the main visual;
- document ingestion graphics.

## 4.9 Whitespace / density

Low density, but not decorative emptiness.

The analysis should feel intentionally focused. Use whitespace around the main semantic progression while keeping the headline and active understanding state visually connected.

## 4.10 Desktop/mobile transformation guidance

Desktop may expose more of the truthful phase sequence at once. Mobile should compress this into current-phase emphasis with restrained completion context.

The underlying Understanding Thread must remain recognizable across both form factors.

## 4.11 CTA prominence

No CTA should compete with analysis. There is no second scan-start action and no secondary onboarding decision.

## 4.12 Must not resemble

- antivirus scanning;
- SEO crawling software;
- an analytics processing dashboard;
- file upload progress;
- a checkout progress meter;
- a generic full-screen spinner;
- a second onboarding wizard step.

## 4.13 Visual acceptance checks

1. The first impression is **brand understanding in progress**, not technical processing.
2. The visual signature supports all four truthful phases **without percentages, fake timers or determinate completion promises**.
3. The screen has one clear focal region rather than multiple equally weighted progress cards.
4. The generic fallback `Building your Brand Preview…` can occupy the same composition without looking broken or empty.
5. The slow-state reassurance can be added without changing the visual family into warning/error treatment.

# 5. Family A — Fast Brand Analysis Mobile 390

## 5.1 Intended user feeling

The same anticipation as Desktop, but more intimate and immediate:

> **Creator Shop is actively getting to know my brand, and I can see what kind of understanding is taking shape.**

## 5.2 Dominant visual moment

The current truthful phase should dominate a compact mobile version of the **Understanding Thread**.

The mobile signature should favour a vertical semantic progression or a current-phase focus with restrained completed-phase history. It must not squeeze a desktop horizontal stepper into 390px.

## 5.3 Hierarchy

1. compact analysis headline;
2. current semantic phase;
3. supporting explanation;
4. completed phase context where truthful;
5. compact brand/domain continuity cue;
6. slow reassurance only when applicable.

## 5.4 Spatial rhythm

Keep the important state visible with minimal scrolling. Avoid placing large decorative content above the active phase.

The composition should feel vertically balanced rather than filling the screen with an oversized loader.

## 5.5 Preferred composition patterns

- compact stacked title + active phase;
- vertical understanding sequence;
- subtle completed-state markers;
- single analysis surface or open-layout treatment;
- no horizontal stepper or carousel.

## 5.6 Card / surface strategy

Prefer a single light surface or open composition. Do not put each phase in a separate mobile card.

## 5.7 Typography emphasis

The current phase should be the strongest non-headline text. Completed phases should remain readable but visually quiet.

Body copy stays at normal Aurora mobile size.

## 5.8 Icon / illustration role

Same restrictions as Desktop. If motion is used, it should be restrained and atmospheric — for example a subtle accent transition along the Understanding Thread — rather than a spinner suggesting indefinite technical work.

## 5.9 Whitespace / density

Compact and calm. Leave enough breathing room for comprehension but avoid a giant empty lower half that makes the experience look unfinished.

## 5.10 Desktop → mobile transformation guidance

Mobile simplifies the visible progression rather than removing the concept:

- current phase becomes dominant;
- completed phases compress;
- brand recognition becomes smaller;
- no loss of semantic continuity;
- slow-state copy expands naturally below the same focal region.

## 5.11 CTA prominence

No new CTA.

## 5.12 Must not resemble

- a mobile download screen;
- a banking transaction progress screen;
- a technical loader;
- a four-step wizard;
- an error state when analysis becomes slow.

## 5.13 Visual acceptance checks

1. At 390px, the current truthful phase is immediately readable without a cramped horizontal stepper.
2. The screen visibly belongs to the same visual family as Desktop while being compositionally redesigned for mobile.
3. The main visual is semantic progression, not a generic spinner/loader.
4. Slow reassurance can expand in-place without overwhelming or changing the state into an alert.
5. The generic fallback remains visually intentional when no phase detail exists.

# 6. Family B — Analysis Recovery Desktop

## 6.1 Intended user feeling

The user should feel:

```text
Creator Shop is being transparent
→ it chose not to guess
→ my brand has not been misrepresented
→ there is a clear way forward
```

Recovery is a **trust moment**, not a failure spectacle.

## 6.2 Dominant visual moment

The dominant moment is the trust statement embedded in the frozen recovery copy — especially the idea that Creator Shop did not fabricate a Preview.

Visually, the page should communicate **restraint with integrity**.

Recommended signature:

### The Trust Boundary

Use a calm, contained focal composition where the state message sits beside or beneath a restrained semantic trust marker. The marker should suggest pause/protection/grounding rather than danger.

The composition should feel like Creator Shop has reached a responsible boundary and is telling the user plainly what happened.

## 6.3 Hierarchy

1. state headline;
2. explanation / non-fabrication trust message;
3. primary `Try again` action;
4. compact brand/domain recognition;
5. lower-emphasis support copy.

Do not visually bury the explanatory body beneath the CTA; the reason for stopping is part of the trust value.

## 6.4 Spatial rhythm

Use a contained, calm central composition with enough breathing room to reduce alarm.

The reading rhythm should be:

```text
recognize context
→ understand why Creator Shop stopped
→ see one clear next action
→ read optional support
```

## 6.5 Preferred composition patterns

- compact brand/domain context near the focal block;
- one trust/recovery focal surface;
- a semantic marker or icon with restrained status emphasis;
- one obvious action;
- explanation visible without disclosure.

The recoverable-failure and Preview-not-ready variants must fit the same structure.

## 6.6 Card / surface strategy

One principal recovery surface is appropriate. Keep it visually related to the Fast Analysis / Brand Preview onboarding world.

Avoid multiple troubleshooting cards, accordions or diagnostic subpanels.

## 6.7 Typography emphasis

- headline calm and direct, not alarmingly oversized;
- body copy prominent enough to carry the trust message;
- CTA clearly primary;
- support copy visually secondary;
- brand/domain context tertiary.

## 6.8 Icon / illustration role

Use a restrained semantic symbol only if it reinforces trust or a responsible pause.

Prefer concepts such as:

- shield/check boundary;
- grounded information marker;
- restrained pause/protection motif.

Avoid:

- red warning triangle as default;
- broken robot;
- server/cloud failure art;
- sad-face illustration;
- bug icon;
- disconnected cable;
- empty-state cartoon.

Semantic variation between recoverable failure and insufficient evidence may use subtle icon/status treatment, not a new layout.

## 6.9 Whitespace / density

Low-to-medium density. Recovery should feel composed and deliberate, not empty.

Do not stretch a short state across a massive desktop canvas with excessive whitespace.

## 6.10 Desktop/mobile transformation guidance

Desktop can use a wider, balanced focal arrangement. Mobile should collapse it into a direct reading sequence while keeping explanation and action prominent.

The Trust Boundary should survive on both.

## 6.11 CTA prominence

`Try again` is the single clear action and should be visually dominant after the explanation.

Do not turn it into a giant conversion CTA; it is a calm recovery action.

## 6.12 Must not resemble

- a 404 page;
- a red system-error page;
- a support/troubleshooting centre;
- a browser/network failure;
- a technical console;
- an empty Preview with missing cards;
- a generic SaaS empty state.

## 6.13 Visual acceptance checks

1. The first emotional read is **transparent and trustworthy**, not alarming or broken.
2. The explanation that Creator Shop did not fill the Preview with guesses remains visually important, not fine print.
3. `Try again` is the single obvious action with no competing troubleshooting UI.
4. The same composition can render both recoverable-failure and Preview-not-ready copy without redesign.
5. No technical/error-console motif, red-error-page convention or fabricated partial Preview appears.

# 7. Family B — Analysis Recovery Mobile 390

## 7.1 Intended user feeling

> **Creator Shop stopped responsibly, explained why, and gives me one straightforward way to continue.**

Mobile should feel particularly calm because a full-screen error treatment can otherwise feel disproportionately severe.

## 7.2 Dominant visual moment

The headline + trust explanation + `Try again` should form one compact focal sequence.

The Trust Boundary marker, if used, should remain small and supportive rather than becoming a large empty-state illustration.

## 7.3 Hierarchy

1. clear headline;
2. fully readable explanation;
3. `Try again`;
4. compact brand/domain context;
5. support copy.

## 7.4 Spatial rhythm

Avoid centering everything vertically with large unused space. Use a top-to-middle reading flow that feels consistent with onboarding.

Keep the action close enough to the explanation that cause and next step read as one thought.

## 7.5 Preferred composition patterns

- compact identity/context cue;
- single recovery surface or open-layout focal block;
- visible explanatory copy;
- full-width or appropriately prominent primary button;
- no disclosure panels.

## 7.6 Card / surface strategy

One light recovery surface maximum. Avoid nesting support copy or brand context into separate cards.

## 7.7 Typography emphasis

Use normal mobile body sizing. Headline should be strong but not take over the viewport. The non-fabrication explanation must remain comfortable to read.

## 7.8 Icon / illustration role

A small trust/pause semantic icon is acceptable. Avoid giant failure illustrations and red warning conventions.

## 7.9 Whitespace / density

Calm and compact. Maintain touch-safe spacing around the CTA and enough whitespace to separate explanation from support copy without producing an empty-state feel.

## 7.10 Desktop → mobile transformation guidance

- remove unnecessary lateral composition;
- keep trust explanation above the action;
- make context compact;
- preserve the same status character;
- do not introduce mobile-only troubleshooting controls.

## 7.11 CTA prominence

`Try again` is visually unmistakable and touch-safe, but not theatrical.

## 7.12 Must not resemble

- a mobile OS/network error;
- a banking transaction failure;
- a red alert sheet;
- a support article;
- a technical retry console.

## 7.13 Visual acceptance checks

1. The entire recovery meaning is understandable without scrolling through decorative or diagnostic content first.
2. The explanation remains visible and readable; it is not hidden behind disclosure or reduced to secondary fine print.
3. `Try again` is the only dominant action and sits naturally after the explanation.
4. The mobile state remains visibly part of the same onboarding/Brand Preview visual world.
5. The composition can swap to Preview-not-ready copy without creating a second recovery family.

# 8. Independent acceptance gates

Every generated reference must pass two independent gates.

## `CONTRACT_FIDELITY`

Owned primarily by Frontend/Product.

Check that the artifact preserves:

- frozen copy verbatim;
- state meaning;
- section/order authority;
- interaction permissions;
- retry/verification semantics;
- variable-density truthfulness;
- pre-verification shell boundary;
- absence of prohibited technical/product controls.

A visual reference that fails contract fidelity cannot be accepted even if aesthetically strong.

## `VISUAL_EXPERIENCE_QUALITY`

Owned primarily by UI/UX Design.

Check that the artifact achieves:

- intended user feeling;
- correct dominant visual moment;
- meaningful hierarchy;
- deliberate rhythm;
- appropriate density;
- coherent but non-repetitive surface strategy;
- readable bounded copy;
- responsive transformation quality;
- Creator Shop specificity rather than generic SaaS convention.

A contract-complete screen that still looks like a report, loader, dashboard or error template does **not** pass visual acceptance.

Use the established outcomes:

```text
VISUAL_ACCEPT
VISUAL_ACCEPT_WITH_IMPLEMENTATION_NOTES
VISUAL_CORRECTION_REQUIRED
VISUAL_RETHINK_REQUIRED
DEFERRED_POLISH
```

Delivery rule:

```text
If another iteration is likely to materially improve
hierarchy / comprehension / visual concept
→ correct now.

If another iteration is likely to improve mainly
micro-spacing / ornament / subjective polish
→ record DEFERRED_POLISH and continue.
```

Use Aurora through hierarchy, typography, spacing and restrained surfaces rather than generic SaaS card grids. Reduce card heaviness as the page becomes long; use deliberate chapter spacing and varied item treatments so Audience, Opportunities and Archetypes do not become one endless list of identical rectangles. ## 10. Prepared-prompt drift watch

The existing prepared Stitch prompts are semantically strong and should remain authoritative. The following visual drift risks should be actively supervised during execution rather than solved by rewriting the contracts:

1. **Fast Analysis:** the phrase `progress region` may cause Stitch to default to a conventional progress bar/stepper. Apply the Understanding Thread guidance before generation.
2. **Brand Preview Mobile:** `stack ... full width` may be interpreted as repeating the exact Desktop card style vertically. Explicitly require reduced card heaviness and varied semantic treatments.
3. **Recovery:** `semantic icon/status emphasis` may pull Stitch toward conventional warning/error iconography. Explicitly frame the state as a Trust Boundary and reject red-error-page defaults.
4. **Accepted Desktop lineage:** prompts that say `using the accepted Desktop as the visual source` should mean visual-language continuity, not pixel/layout cloning. Mobile must transform the experience perceptually.
5. **Healthcare fixture:** continue to prevent the Northstar content from producing clinical colours, medical iconography or healthcare-specific theming; Aurora and Creator Shop remain the visual authority.
