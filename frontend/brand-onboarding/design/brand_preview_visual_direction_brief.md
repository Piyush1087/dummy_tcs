# Brand Preview — Visual Experience Direction Brief

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** PRODUCT-DIRECTED VISUAL AUTHORITY FOR STITCH / FUTURE REDESIGN  
**Owner:** UI/UX Design AI Worker  
**Scope:** Continuous Fast Brand Analysis, Brand Preview, Analysis Recovery  
**Frozen semantic authority remains unchanged.**

## 1. Why this brief exists

The Brand Preview contracts correctly define meaning, copy, state and interaction, but those contracts alone do not fully define the intended **experience quality**.

A semantically correct screen can still feel like:

- an extraction report;
- a generic SaaS page;
- a document with cards;
- an administrative review surface.

That is not the intended experience.

This brief defines the visual character and hierarchy Stitch should understand before generating or refining Brand Preview-family screens.

It does **not** reopen Product, copy, Intelligence, Data Extraction or Frontend state authority.

---

## 2. Core experience objective

Brand Preview is the user's first substantial reward after Creator Shop has analysed their business.

The desired reaction is:

> **“Creator Shop already understands my business and has an intelligent, useful point of view about how creators can help.”**

The user should not feel:

> “Creator Shop generated a report from my website.”

The experience should create a sense of **recognition, insight and strategic possibility**, then naturally invite the user to continue.

---

## 3. Experience character

Brand Preview should feel:

```text
intelligent
premium
editorial
human
calm
personalized
strategic
confident
```

It should **not** feel:

```text
dashboard-like
report-like
form-like
wizard-like
clinical
corporate-consulting-slide-like
generic SaaS
```

Aurora remains the design-system authority. “Premium” should come from hierarchy, composition, typography, spacing, surface restraint and visual rhythm — not decorative excess.

---

## 4. Emotional / perceptual sequence

The page should progress perceptually as:

```text
RECOGNITION
“We found the right brand.”
        ↓
UNDERSTANDING
“They understand what my brand means.”
        ↓
AUDIENCE CLARITY
“They understand who I need to influence.”
        ↓
STRATEGIC POSSIBILITY
“They can see where creators could actually help.”
        ↓
CREATOR STARTING POINT
“They already know what kind of creators might fit.”
        ↓
CONTINUATION
“I want to claim this and keep going.”
```

The visual design should reinforce this progression rather than present five equally weighted report sections.

---

## 5. Visual hierarchy

### Tier 1 — Page reveal

`Your Brand Preview` and the page headline should feel like a meaningful reveal after analysis, not a standard page heading.

The top of the page should quickly communicate:

- analysis is complete enough to show value;
- the identified brand is recognizable;
- the user is entering a personalized result.

Avoid excessive empty space before the first meaningful content.

### Tier 2 — Primary insight reveal

`How we understand your brand` is the primary intellectual payoff and should be the **strongest insight surface on the page**.

It should feel more important than metadata and more distinctive than repeated cards.

The user should be able to visually identify this as:

> “This is Creator Shop's point of view.”

### Tier 3 — Audience understanding

Audience groups should feel like **strategic cohorts**, not a database list and not generic persona cards.

The visual language should make each cohort quickly recognizable while keeping explanations readable.

### Tier 4 — Creator opportunity

`Where creators can make the difference` should be the strongest **strategy/actionable** section.

This is where the Preview moves from understanding the brand to demonstrating creator-marketing intelligence.

Opportunity treatment may carry stronger visual emphasis than Audience, provided it does not become campaign configuration.

### Tier 5 — Creator archetypes

`Creators we'd start with` should feel more **human and creator-oriented** than plain report rows.

For the current phase, use typography/iconography/surface treatment rather than runtime AI-generated persona imagery.

Future direction may introduce a controlled archetype illustration/persona system, but that is explicitly deferred.

### Tier 6 — Verification continuation

The verification action should feel like the natural conclusion of the value story.

It should not feel like:

- an unrelated marketing footer;
- a giant detached banner;
- a hard-sell conversion block.

The user should feel they are continuing the experience they just saw.

---

## 6. Brand recognition treatment

`The brand we found` is a trust/recognition moment, not an administrative metadata section.

Preferred qualities:

- compact;
- recognizable;
- polished;
- identity-led;
- visually lighter than the primary insight.

Brand name should dominate the recognition block.

Industry/domain/descriptor are supporting context.

Avoid:

- table-like field/value presentation;
- excessive labels;
- large empty metadata zones;
- making absence of logo feel like an error.

---

## 7. Surface / card strategy

Do not put every semantic unit inside the same rectangular card style.

The page should use **varied but coherent composition**:

- one strong insight surface;
- cohort treatment for Audience;
- strategic opportunity treatment;
- creator-profile/archetype treatment;
- restrained recognition block.

Variation should communicate meaning, not decoration.

Avoid nested card-on-card patterns and repeated three-column SaaS tiles across every section.

---

## 8. Typography and editorial character

Typography should do substantial visual work.

Use Aurora typography literally, with hierarchy strong enough to distinguish:

- reveal headline;
- primary insight;
- section title;
- item title;
- explanation;
- contextual metadata.

Long bounded prose should remain comfortable to read.

Do not shrink body type to preserve card geometry.

Editorial does **not** mean magazine styling or ornamental serif experimentation outside Aurora. It means thoughtful reading hierarchy and intentional content pacing.

---

## 9. Whitespace and rhythm

Whitespace should create pacing, but not make the page feel sparse or unfinished.

Preferred rhythm:

```text
reveal
→ compact recognition
→ strong insight anchor
→ clear strategic sections
→ integrated continuation
```

Avoid very large dead zones between sections merely to make the page feel premium.

Use section spacing, surface transitions and typography to create rhythm.

---

## 10. Color and visual emphasis

Use Aurora accents intentionally.

The screen should not be mostly monochrome with occasional arbitrary green decoration.

Use accent/surface roles to indicate:

- primary insight;
- strategic opportunities;
- creator-oriented elements;
- action hierarchy.

Avoid:

- decorative gradients without semantic role;
- healthcare-specific colors/themes because the primary fixture is Healthcare;
- overuse of dark full-width panels;
- excessive status colors.

---

## 11. Iconography / illustration direction

Icons should clarify semantic categories rather than merely decorate cards.

Current Brand Preview may use restrained icons for:

- audience context;
- opportunity role;
- creator archetype character.

Do not use generic icons if they do not improve recognition.

### Deferred future direction

A later UI redesign may introduce a curated AI-generated archetype/persona illustration library.

If pursued later:

- imagery should be curated rather than generated live per onboarding run;
- a fixed house style should be used;
- images should represent archetype character, not imply a real person or guaranteed demographic;
- the visual system should remain reusable across Industries.

This is not required for current MVP Stitch screens.

---

## 12. Content density / copy envelope guidance

The current Brand Preview copy contract is already frozen and must be preserved for this phase.

For future UI/UX work, use visual direction **before** final copy freeze and define copy budgets early.

Future preferred envelope should trend toward:

- primary Brand insight: concise enough to remain one strong feature block;
- Audience: compact cohort title + short rationale;
- Opportunities: concise strategic title + one useful explanation;
- Archetypes: memorable label + short fit rationale.

The principle is:

> **The design should not become a container for unconstrained prose. Copy and visual hierarchy should be co-designed.**

Do not change the current frozen copy merely to satisfy this future principle.

---

## 13. Variable-density behavior

The visual concept must remain intentional for:

- 1 Audience;
- 1 Opportunity;
- 1 Archetype;
- missing logo;
- missing descriptor.

A reduced-density Preview should look **deliberately concise**, not incomplete.

Never preserve grid symmetry using:

- empty cards;
- placeholders;
- filler content;
- oversized whitespace.

---

## 14. Desktop direction

Desktop should feel expansive enough to create a premium reveal, but should not use width simply to create dashboard grids.

Good uses of horizontal space include:

- strong editorial insight block;
- balanced strategic cohort/opportunity layouts;
- visual contrast between recognition, understanding and strategy.

Avoid repeating identical 3-up card rows for Audience, Opportunities and Archetypes.

---

## 15. Mobile direction

Mobile should preserve the emotional sequence, not merely stack every Desktop card.

Priorities:

- meaningful reveal above the fold;
- compact brand recognition;
- strong insight block early;
- clear one-column strategic rhythm;
- readable full copy;
- reduced visual heaviness;
- no horizontal carousel as canonical content architecture;
- verification action remains clear without dominating the entire scroll.

A sticky CTA may be explored only as the same canonical action and only if it improves usability without competing with the Preview story.

---

## 16. Fast Brand Analysis family direction

Fast Brand Analysis should inherit the same premium/calm visual language but be much lighter than Brand Preview.

It should feel like:

> **Creator Shop is actively forming an understanding of my brand.**

Not:

> **A technical scan is running.**

Visual qualities:

- calm focus;
- minimal content;
- clear current activity;
- gentle progression;
- brand recognition cue where helpful;
- restrained motion;
- no technical diagnostics.

The analysis shell should visually build anticipation for the Brand Preview reveal.

---

## 17. Recovery family direction

Recovery should remain part of the same onboarding visual world.

It should feel:

- trustworthy;
- calm;
- transparent;
- recoverable where appropriate;
- not alarming unless the semantic state requires it.

Avoid generic red error-page treatment.

The user should understand that Creator Shop refused to fabricate an ungrounded Preview — this is a trust signal, not merely a failure.

---

## 18. Stitch freedom

Stitch may explore:

- composition within the frozen section order;
- surface hierarchy;
- editorial grouping;
- visual accents;
- icons;
- rhythm;
- responsive transformation;
- restrained animation treatment for analysis;
- optional mobile sticky equivalent of the canonical CTA.

Stitch must not change:

- Product semantics;
- frozen copy;
- section order;
- CTA meaning;
- state meaning;
- editability;
- variable-density truthfulness;
- pre-verification shell boundary.

---

## 19. Visual anti-patterns

Reject or correct designs that become:

- a static report/document;
- five similarly weighted sections separated only by dividers;
- repetitive card grids;
- an authenticated dashboard;
- a form/review workflow;
- overly sparse with large dead spaces;
- a marketing landing page with a detached conversion banner;
- healthcare-themed because of the fixture;
- visually rich but semantically generic;
- polished yet lacking a clear primary insight moment.

---

## 20. Current D03 status and application of this brief

The current Brand Preview Desktop reference has improved enough to continue the current Stitch execution with **deferred visual polish** rather than spending unlimited iterations on D03.

This brief should therefore be used in two ways:

1. **Immediately:** feed it to Stitch before generating the next Brand Preview family reference so Mobile and later Analysis/Recovery inherit the intended experience character rather than only the structural contracts.
2. **Later redesign phase:** use this brief as the starting visual authority when Brand Preview receives a dedicated aesthetics/UI refinement pass.

Do not reinterpret this brief as a mandate to reopen the frozen Brand Preview semantic/copy/state contracts during the current execution.

---

## 21. Visual acceptance bar

A future accepted Brand Preview design should pass both:

### Contract fidelity

All frozen semantics/copy/actions are preserved.

### Experience quality

The screen should convincingly communicate:

```text
Creator Shop recognized me
        ↓
Creator Shop understands me
        ↓
Creator Shop has a creator strategy point of view
        ↓
I want to continue
```

If a design is semantically correct but still reads as a report, generic SaaS page or administrative review, visual correction is required.
